5. Badges, tags, and status indicators

These are the most frequently scanned elements in the application. An operator glances at a screen of 50 rows and needs to spot the three that need attention. The badge system must make that instant.

Structure

Every badge is a small pill: display: inline-flex; align-items: center; gap: 4px; padding: 3px 10px; border-radius: 5px; font-size: 11px; font-weight: 600; white-space: nowrap;

Semantic color mappings (tinted background + dark text from same hue)
Status/meaning	Background	Text	Example use
Success/completed	green-50 (soft green)	green-800 (dark green)	"Completed", "Cleared", "Verde"
Active/in-progress	blue-50 (soft blue)	blue-800 (dark blue)	"In yard", "In transit", "Active"
Scheduled/planned	purple-50 (soft purple)	purple-800 (dark purple)	"Planned", "Checked in", "Booked"
Warning/attention	amber-50 (soft amber)	amber-800 (dark amber)	"At position", "Naranja", "Quality hold"
Danger/blocked	red-50 (soft red)	red-800 (dark red)	"Breached", "Rojo", "Customs hold"
Neutral/default	gray-100 (surface tint)	gray-700 (secondary)	"In queue", "Manual", "Pending"
Rules
Badge text is always the same color family as the badge background, just the darkest stop in the ramp. Never use white text on these tinted badges — they are meant to be subtle, not buttons.
Never combine two semantic colors in one badge. If a badge needs an icon, the icon inherits the text color.
Equipment badges (Crane, Manual, Yard Truck, RTG) use a leading icon and neutral or category-appropriate tint. These are classification labels, not status indicators.
Maximum two badges per cell, on one line. If a cell has both an equipment badge and a status badge, they sit side by side with 6px gap. Never stack badges vertically.
6. Route and coordinate formatting

Bay/row/tier coordinates and route paths are among the most complex strings in the application. They must be formatted for instant parsing, not just displayed as raw text.

Coordinate format: Bay 9 · R1 · T3
Monospace, 13px, weight 500
Separator dots (·) in muted color — they provide rhythm, not information
"Bay" prefix in normal weight, the number in bold weight if you want to emphasize it, but consistency matters more than emphasis
Route arrows: Origin → Destination
The arrow (→) gets 6–8px horizontal padding on each side
Arrow color: --text-muted
Arrow size: 14px (slightly larger than surrounding text so it reads as a divider)
Origin and destination blocks should have equal visual weight unless one is a special location
Special locations: GATE
Render as a mini pill: background: var(--surface-1); padding: 2px 8px; border-radius: 4px; font-size: 12px; font-weight: 600; text-transform: uppercase;
This distinguishes "GATE" from bay coordinates at a glance — the operator knows immediately this move touches the facility boundary
